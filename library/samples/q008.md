# Task 1 · Sample 5: Rain-shadow deserts

*The diagram below shows the formation of rain-shadow deserts. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.*

![Task 1 chart](https://engnovatewebsitestorage.blob.core.windows.net/ielts-writing-task-1-images/ee4bc97893b4b4c1)

Grouping: stages 1 to 4 (air rises and clouds form) / stages 5 to 7 (rain falls and dry air moves inland). All seven stages must appear.

**Model answer — 183 words**

*The diagram illustrates how rain-shadow deserts are formed on the inland side of a coastal mountain range.*

*Overall, the process consists of seven stages and is driven entirely by the movement of moist air over high ground. It is also clear that the two sides of the mountain receive very different amounts of rainfall, which is what produces the desert.*

*The process begins when winds carrying moisture from the sea approach the coast. As the land rises, these winds are forced upwards along the windward side of the mountains. The air cools as it gains altitude, and the water vapour it carries condenses into clouds near the summit.*

*Rain then falls on the windward slope, which removes most of the remaining moisture from the air. The dry air continues over the peak and descends the leeward side, where no further rainfall occurs. Finally, these dry winds travel inland for thousands of kilometres. Because the land they cross receives almost no precipitation, a rain-shadow desert forms on this side of the range.*
