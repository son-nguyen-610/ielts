# Task 2 · Sample 6: Tourism and local culture

*Some people believe that international tourism has a negative impact on local cultures and traditions. Others believe that tourism helps preserve them. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.*

**Model answer — 338 words**

*Proponents of international tourism have long argued that it sustains the traditions of the places it reaches. However, critics contend that visitors gradually erode the cultures they travel to see. From my perspective, tourism preserves local culture more often than it damages it, largely because preservation requires funding that few communities could otherwise generate.*

*One primary reason is that tourist revenue makes traditional practices economically viable. In many regions, crafts, music and festivals had been in decline because younger generations could not earn a living from them. When visitors create demand, these skills become a source of income and are therefore passed on rather than abandoned. For example, craft skills that had almost disappeared from rural areas are now practised full time again, simply because visitors buy what local households had stopped buying.*

*Nonetheless, the concerns of critics are well founded. Where visitor numbers are very large, traditions are frequently adapted into shortened performances designed for audiences rather than for the communities themselves. Without limits on scale, religious sites become attractions and residents are priced out of districts their families have occupied for generations. For example, a district where short-term rentals outnumber permanent homes eventually loses the residents whose daily life the visitors came to observe.*

*That said, the damage described above results from unmanaged tourism rather than from tourism itself. Visitor caps, entry fees directed towards restoration, and restrictions on short-term rentals allow communities to retain both the revenue and the sites that generate it. Once such measures are in place, tourist income funds precisely the conservation work that critics demand. Destinations that admit fewer visitors at higher prices consistently fund more restoration work than those competing purely on volume.*

*In conclusion, the cultural benefits of international tourism outweigh its costs where visitor numbers are regulated, because the revenue it generates is what makes preservation affordable. Ideally, governments should treat tourism as a source of conservation funding rather than as an industry to be expanded without limit.*
