# #11 · Task 2: Ageing population

*In many countries, people are living longer than ever before. What problems can this cause for individuals and society? What measures could be taken to address these problems?*

Đề problem–solution. A = kéo dài tuổi lao động · B = tăng chi công · Body 3 = Fix. Câu 1 mở bài tả tình hình, câu 2 nói vì sao là vấn đề.

**Model answer — 334 words**

*Life expectancy has risen steadily across most of the world over the past half century. Although longer life is desirable in itself, it places sustained pressure on pension and healthcare systems that were designed for a far younger population. From my perspective, the central problem is the falling ratio of working-age adults to dependents, and the most effective response is to extend working life rather than simply to increase public spending.*

*The principal difficulty is financial rather than medical. In any country where birth rates fell decades ago, each working adult now supports far more retired citizens than a single worker once did, which means pension and healthcare costs fall on a shrinking tax base. As a result, governments must either raise taxes on a smaller workforce or reduce the benefits on which older citizens depend. At the individual level, this pressure falls on adults in middle age, who increasingly support both their children and their parents at the same time.*

*The most common response is to increase public spending on healthcare and pensions. Additional funding does relieve immediate hardship, as it allows hospitals to expand geriatric services and keeps pension payments above subsistence level. Without such spending, poverty among the elderly rises quickly. However, this measure addresses the symptom rather than the cause, because the revenue required must still be generated by a workforce that continues to contract.*

*That said, extending working life addresses the problem at its source. Raising the retirement age, combined with retraining programmes for older employees, increases the number of contributors while simultaneously reducing the number of claimants. Countries that have already raised the pension age report smaller funding shortfalls than those that relied on higher spending alone.*

*In conclusion, the benefits of extending working life outweigh those of increased spending alone, because only the former corrects the imbalance between contributors and dependents. Ideally, governments should combine a gradual rise in the retirement age with retraining and flexible employment, so that older workers remain productive without being forced into unsuitable roles.*
