# #6 · Task 2: Working from home

*Computers and modems have made it possible for office workers to do much of their work from home instead of working in offices every day. Working from home should be encouraged as it is good for workers and employers. To what extent do you agree?*

A = home working · B = office attendance · Body 3 = Fix.

**Model answer — 326 words**

*Proponents of remote working have long championed its flexibility for employees and its cost savings for firms. However, critics contend that physical separation weakens the coordination on which organisations depend. From my perspective, home working deserves encouragement, largely because the problems it creates can be managed, whereas the gains cannot be obtained in any other way.*

*One primary reason is the reduction in commuting time. In most large cities, employees spend between one and two hours each day travelling to and from an office, frequently in congested conditions. When that time is returned to them, fatigue decreases and the hours available for both work and rest increase, which raises productivity and reduces staff turnover. For example, firms that adopted remote arrangements after 2020 consistently reported lower resignation rates among employees with long commutes.*

*Nonetheless, office attendance has undeniable merits of its own. Informal contact between colleagues allows problems to be identified and resolved before they are formally raised, and new employees acquire procedures largely by observing experienced staff. Without regular contact of this kind, junior employees develop more slowly and teams coordinate less effectively. For example, organisations that moved entirely online reported that training periods for new recruits lengthened considerably.*

*That said, these difficulties are procedural rather than fundamental. Scheduled in-person meetings, structured handovers and supervised onboarding address the specific gaps that distance creates, and most organisations have already adopted hybrid arrangements for this purpose. Once such measures are in place, the coordination problem described above is largely resolved, while the reduction in commuting time remains.*

*In conclusion, the benefits of home working outweigh the organisational difficulties it introduces, because those difficulties can be managed through hybrid scheduling. Ideally, employers should permit remote work by default and reserve office attendance for tasks that genuinely require collaboration.*
