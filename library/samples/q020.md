# Task 2 · Sample 12: AI replacing human workers

*Artificial intelligence is increasingly used to perform tasks that were previously carried out by human workers. Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?*

**Model answer — 336 words**

*Proponents of automation have long argued that machines should perform work that people find repetitive or dangerous. However, critics contend that the current wave of artificial intelligence eliminates employment faster than any economy can replace it. From my perspective, the advantages are greater, largely because the disruption is a question of transition speed rather than of permanent job scarcity.*

*One primary reason is the removal of work that damages the people who perform it. Warehouse lifting, night-shift monitoring and repetitive data entry produce measurable injury and fatigue, and employers in most countries struggle to fill such positions at all. When software or machinery absorbs these tasks, the same output is achieved without the physical cost. For example, automated inspection in manufacturing has reduced both defect rates and the repetitive strain injuries that inspection work used to cause.*

*Nonetheless, the concerns about displacement are serious. Unlike earlier automation, current systems affect clerical and professional work that previously appeared secure, and they do so within a few years rather than across a generation. Workers in their forties and fifties cannot reasonably be expected to retrain into unfamiliar fields at short notice. Without intervention, the gains accrue to employers while the costs fall on individuals who did nothing to cause them.*

*Even so, this objection identifies a problem of pace rather than of direction. Every previous technology that eliminated occupations also created categories of work that had not existed before, and employment levels recovered once training systems adjusted. Funded retraining, income support during transition and phased adoption in affected sectors address the specific difficulty described above, which is that displacement arrives faster than individuals can respond to it.*

*In conclusion, the benefits of artificial intelligence outweigh the disruption it causes, because that disruption concerns the speed of transition rather than the permanent disappearance of work. Ideally, governments should require employers adopting these systems to contribute to the retraining of the workers they replace.*
