# Task 1 · Sample 12: Homework hours and science scores

*The bar charts show education data related to young adults aged 15 years in 10 different countries in 2015. The first chart shows in which countries adolescents do the most homework in terms of hours per week. The second chart shows the nations that scored the best on an international science test. Summarise the data by selecting and reporting the main features and make comparisons where relevant.*

**Model answer — 204 words**

*The two charts compare the weekly homework hours of fifteen-year-olds in ten countries with the ten highest mean scores on an international science test, both recorded in 2015.*

*Overall, the countries whose students spent the most time on homework were largely absent from the list of highest-scoring nations. It is also clear that the country reporting the fewest homework hours achieved the best science result of all.*

*Italian students devoted the most time to homework, at 8.6 hours per week, followed by Ireland at 7.2 and Poland at 6.5. Four further countries clustered between 5.1 and 6.1 hours, namely Mexico, Canada, Australia and the United States. At the opposite end, Japanese students spent 4.3 hours, while South Korea and Finland recorded the lowest figures, at 2.9 and 2.8 respectively.*

*The science results correspond poorly with these figures. Finland achieved the highest mean score, at 568, despite spending the least time on homework, and Japan ranked sixth at 531 with comparatively few hours. None of the three countries with the heaviest homework loads appeared among the top ten scorers at all. The United States, meanwhile, combined above-average homework hours with the lowest score in the table, at 516.*
