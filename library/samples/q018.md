# #18 · Task 2: Self-medication instead of doctors

*Nowadays many people treat themselves with medicines bought online or over the counter rather than visiting a doctor. What problems does this cause, and what measures could be taken to address them?*

Đề problem–solution. A = mở rộng tiếp cận chuyên môn (khám từ xa, siết bán thuốc) · B = chiến dịch nâng cao nhận thức · Body 3 = Fix.

**Model answer — 329 words**

*Medicines that once required a prescription can now be obtained online within minutes. Although self-treatment saves time and money, it places people in the position of diagnosing conditions they have no training to identify. From my perspective, the most serious consequence is the delayed detection of illness, and the most effective response is to make professional consultation easier rather than merely to warn against the alternative.*

*The principal danger is that self-treatment suppresses symptoms without addressing their cause. A patient who treats persistent pain with painkillers may relieve the discomfort while the underlying condition continues to develop unobserved. By the time a doctor is consulted, treatment options have narrowed and costs have risen. Inappropriate use of antibiotics compounds the problem further, as incomplete courses taken without supervision accelerate the resistance that makes these medicines less effective for everyone.*

*The most common response is to run public awareness campaigns warning against self-medication. Such campaigns do reach large audiences cheaply, and they establish which symptoms genuinely require professional attention. Without that information, patients cannot judge when self-treatment becomes dangerous. However, awareness alone changes little, because most people who avoid doctors already understand the risk and are deterred by cost, waiting times or distance instead.*

*That said, removing those obstacles addresses the behaviour at its source. Remote consultation allows a patient to reach a physician within minutes at a fraction of the cost of an appointment, which eliminates precisely the barriers that drive people online in the first place. Requiring pharmacies to verify prescriptions for high-risk categories reinforces this by restricting the supply that makes unsupervised treatment possible.*

*In conclusion, the benefits of improving access to professional care outweigh those of awareness campaigns alone, because the problem stems from inconvenience rather than ignorance. Ideally, governments should expand remote consultation services while tightening the rules on which medicines may be sold without a prescription.*
