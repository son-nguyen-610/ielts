# #2 · Task 2: Both parents working

*In today's competitive world, many families find it necessary for both parents to go out to work. While some say the children in these families benefit from the additional income, others feel they lack support because of their parents' absence. Which viewpoint do you agree with?*

**Model answer — 338 words**

*Proponents of dual-income households have long championed the financial security that two salaries provide. However, critics contend that this security is obtained at the expense of parental supervision. From my perspective, the additional income is more important, largely because the supervision these critics value is difficult to provide in a household under financial pressure.*

*One primary reason is a second income's capacity to remove material deprivation during childhood. In many families, a single wage covers housing and food but leaves no margin for school fees, medical treatment or adequate study conditions. A second income eliminates this shortfall, which allows children to remain in education and reduces the financial instability affecting the household as a whole. For example, a household with two incomes can fund tutoring, dental treatment and school expenses that a single wage would never stretch to.*

*Nonetheless, parental presence has undeniable merits of its own. Children who are unsupervised after school have no immediate access to an adult when difficulties arise, and adolescence is the period in which this absence has the greatest consequences. Without an adult who observes changes in behaviour, early problems remain undetected until they become serious. For example, adolescents who spend extended periods without supervision are consistently more likely to engage in harmful behaviour.*

*That said, consistent parental attention is difficult to sustain on an inadequate income. Financial insecurity does not disappear when a parent remains at home, and it converts available time into disputes over household expenses. Once basic costs are covered, the remaining time can be used for communication rather than crisis management, which means the supervision children require depends on the stability that a second income provides.*

*In conclusion, the benefits of a second income outweigh the costs of parental absence, because financial stability creates the conditions in which effective parenting is possible. Ideally, parents should earn what the household requires while protecting the periods in which supervision is actually provided.*
