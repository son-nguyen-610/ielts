# #9 · Task 1: Cycling versus driving

*The charts below show the reasons people give for cycling to work and for driving to work. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.*

Grouping: chart 1 / chart 2. The contrast worth stating in the overview is that cycling reasons are evenly spread while driving is dominated by one reason.

**Model answer — 188 words**

*The two pie charts compare the reasons people give for cycling to work with the reasons given for driving.*

*Overall, the reasons for cycling are distributed fairly evenly, whereas a single reason dominates the responses for driving. It is also clear that cyclists are motivated mainly by health and environmental concerns, while drivers are motivated by convenience.*

*Among cyclists, health and fitness and reduced pollution were the most common reasons, each selected by 30% of respondents. The absence of parking difficulties accounted for a further 15%, and the absence of costs for 13%. Speed was the least common reason, cited by only 12%, which suggests that cyclists do not generally regard their journey as the quickest option.*

*Among drivers, comfort was by far the most frequent reason, at 40%, almost twice the figure for the next most common response. Distance to work accounted for 21%, while speed and the need to carry items were each selected by 14%. Safety was the least cited reason, at 11%, making it the only category below the equivalent minimum among cyclists.*
