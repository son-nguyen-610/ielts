# Task 2 · Sample 2: Both parents working, opposite side

Same prompt as 2. Use the two together to show that the skeleton does not favour a side, and as the worked example of the Flaw turn.

A = parental presence · B = the additional income · Body 3 = Flaw.

**Model answer — 336 words**

*Proponents of dual-income households have long championed the financial security that two salaries provide. However, critics contend that this security is obtained at the expense of parental supervision. From my perspective, parental presence deserves greater priority, largely because the problems it prevents cannot be resolved through additional income.*

*One primary reason is a parent's capacity to identify difficulties before they become severe. Adolescents rarely report problems directly, and changes in mood, academic performance and social behaviour develop gradually. An adult who is regularly present observes these changes at an early stage, when intervention is still straightforward. For example, teachers frequently report that students who fall behind academically do so over several months before anyone at home identifies the decline.*

*Nonetheless, a second income has undeniable merits of its own. For households near subsistence level, a single wage covers housing and food but not school fees, medical treatment or adequate study conditions. Without a second wage, children leave education for financial reasons rather than by choice, and parental attention cannot reverse this outcome. For example, children in households near subsistence still leave school in their early teens because the family needs a second wage.*

*Even so, additional income produces diminishing returns once basic needs are met. Beyond that point, further working hours fund supplementary services such as private tutoring and improved accommodation, while reducing the supervision for which no substitute exists. A child may receive extensive private tuition and still have no adult available when difficulties arise, which is precisely the failure described above.*

*In conclusion, for households above subsistence level, the benefits of parental presence outweigh those of additional income, because supervision cannot be provided retrospectively. Ideally, parents should earn what the household genuinely requires and limit working hours beyond that level.*
