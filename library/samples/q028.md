# #28 · Task 2: Fewer people cycling to work

*Fewer people are using bicycles as a means of transport. Why is this, and how could cycling be encouraged?*

**Model answer**

*Cycling has lost ground as a way of getting to work in many cities. The decline comes less from any dislike of the bicycle than from the conditions surrounding it. Reversing it depends on changing those conditions rather than on persuading people to try harder.*

*One primary reason is the perceived danger of sharing road space with motor traffic. Most urban roads were designed around the car, with cycling added afterwards wherever room allowed. A cyclist who must ride within a metre of moving traffic accepts a risk no other commuter is asked to take, and most people decline it. A parent deciding how a child will reach school will choose the slower bus over a route that crosses three busy junctions.*

*The cost of that retreat falls on the whole city rather than on cyclists alone. Every journey abandoned on a bicycle reappears as a journey made by car or bus. Those vehicles occupy far more road space for each passenger carried, so congestion worsens and the roads become more hostile still. A street that loses its cyclists gains the traffic that made cycling unpleasant in the first place.*

*The remedy therefore has to begin with the road rather than with the rider. Separated lanes, secure parking and priority at junctions remove the risk directly. Once a route feels safe enough for a child to use, the pool of possible cyclists widens to include almost everyone. A route a ten year old can ride alone is a route most adults will ride as well.*

*In conclusion, the fall in cycling follows directly from roads built around the car, and separated space is what reverses it. Campaigns that ask people to cycle without changing the road ask them to accept a risk they have already judged and rejected.*
