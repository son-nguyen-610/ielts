# Task 2 · Sample 9: Easier international travel

*These days it is much easier for many people to travel to different countries for tourism than in the past. Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?*

A = mặt lợi của việc đi lại dễ hơn · B = mặt hại (quá tải du lịch, khí thải) · Body 3 = Flaw (thiệt hại chỉ tập trung ở một nhóm nhỏ điểm đến, trong khi lợi ích trải rộng).

**Model answer — 325 words**

*Proponents of cheap international travel have long championed the access it gives ordinary people to places once reserved for the wealthy. However, critics contend that this expansion has damaged the destinations it delivers people to. From my perspective, the advantages are greater, largely because the harm is concentrated in a small number of sites while the benefits are spread very widely.*

*One primary reason is the economic opportunity that visitor numbers create outside major cities. In many regions, agriculture and fishing have declined without replacement, and younger residents leave for work elsewhere. Tourism generates employment in accommodation, transport and catering, which allows those residents to remain and allows local governments to collect revenue from visitors rather than from residents. For example, coastal villages that once relied entirely on fishing now support guesthouses, transport and catering businesses that employ the same families year round.*

*Nonetheless, the objections raised against mass tourism are serious. Historic districts built for small resident populations now receive visitor numbers that their streets, housing and water systems were never designed to carry. Without controls, housing is converted into short-term rentals, residents are priced out, and the fabric that attracted visitors begins to deteriorate. Aviation emissions have also risen steadily as fares have fallen.*

*Even so, these problems are confined to a small group of destinations rather than inherent in travel itself. A handful of famous cities absorb the overwhelming majority of complaints about overtourism, while far more regions remain under-visited and would benefit from greater numbers. Because the damage follows concentration rather than volume, measures that disperse visitors, such as regional marketing and seasonal pricing, address the problem described above without reducing access.*

*In conclusion, the benefits of easier international travel outweigh its costs, because the damage is localised while the economic gains reach a far wider range of regions. Ideally, governments should direct visitors towards places that want them and regulate the few destinations that are genuinely overwhelmed.*
