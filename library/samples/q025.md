**Model answer**

*The bar chart compares the share of commuters in a European city who travelled to and from work by four different means in 1960, 1980 and 2000.*

*Overall, the car replaced walking as the dominant form of commuting over the period. Bus use rose and then fell back, while cycling and walking declined throughout.*

*Car use stood at only 7 percent in 1960, the lowest figure recorded by any mode in that year. It then climbed sharply to 25 percent by 1980 and reached 37 percent in 2000, by which point it was the most common choice. Bus travel followed a flatter path, rising from 20 percent to roughly 27 percent before falling back to 17 percent at the end of the period.*

*Walking moved in the opposite direction to the car. It accounted for 35 percent of journeys in 1960 but had dropped to 17 percent by 1980 and to 10 percent by 2000. Cycling declined more gently at first, falling from 27 percent to 22 percent before collapsing to 7 percent in the final year. By 2000 the two modes requiring no vehicle together accounted for less than a fifth of all commuting.*
