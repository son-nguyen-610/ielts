# #13 · Task 2: Parks replaced by housing

*Many cities are replacing traditional public parks and open spaces with new housing. Do the advantages of this development outweigh the disadvantages?*

A = mặt hại (phe mình) · B = mặt lợi · Body 3 = Flaw. Lưu ý thứ tự khi bênh mặt hại, câu cuối kết bài vẫn phải giữ thứ bậc.

**Model answer — 336 words**

*Proponents of building on public parkland have long argued that severe housing shortages justify the use of any available site. However, critics contend that removing open space damages the very neighbourhoods such housing is intended to serve. From my perspective, the disadvantages of this policy are greater, largely because the housing gained can be built elsewhere while the open space cannot be recovered.*

*One primary reason is that urban green space is permanently lost once it is developed. Land within an established city is fixed, and a park converted into housing will remain housing for as long as those buildings stand. Residents therefore lose the only space available for exercise, recreation and relief from summer heat, and this loss falls hardest on households without private gardens. For example, residents of a dense inner district with no park have nowhere to walk, exercise or sit outdoors except the street itself.*

*Nonetheless, the case for additional housing is a serious one. In cities where demand far exceeds supply, rent consumes an increasing share of household income and younger residents are pushed into progressively longer commutes. Without new construction, overcrowding worsens and essential workers are displaced to the periphery. For example, in a city where rent rises faster than wages year after year, each additional dwelling genuinely relieves pressure on the households at the bottom.*

*Even so, parkland is not the only site available. Disused industrial land, vacant office buildings and underused car parks exist in most large cities, and converting them yields comparable numbers of homes without removing public space. Because these alternatives exist, the shortage described above does not require the sacrifice this policy demands, and building on parkland usually reflects the low cost of public land rather than genuine necessity.*

*In conclusion, the disadvantages of replacing parks with housing outweigh the advantages, because the shortage can be addressed through brownfield development while lost open space cannot be restored. Ideally, planning authorities should require that alternative sites be exhausted before any public green space is released for construction.*
