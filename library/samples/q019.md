# Task 2 · Sample 11: Government funding for artists

*Some people believe that governments should spend money supporting artists such as painters, musicians and writers. Others argue that this money would be better spent elsewhere. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.*

**Model answer — 332 words**

*Proponents of public arts funding have long argued that a society's cultural output requires support that the market will not provide. However, critics contend that scarce public money belongs in hospitals and schools instead. From my perspective, governments should continue funding the arts, largely because the sums involved are too small to affect the services that critics wish to protect.*

*One primary reason is that artistic work generates value which its creators cannot capture. A novel read for decades or a public sculpture seen daily benefits far more people than ever pay for it. Private investors fund work expected to return a profit quickly, which systematically excludes experimental and regional work regardless of its quality. For example, almost no national orchestra or public museum anywhere would survive on ticket revenue alone, however popular it is.*

*Nonetheless, the objection deserves serious consideration. Health systems across the world operate with waiting lists measured in months, and schools in many districts lack basic equipment. When budgets are fixed, every sum allocated to a gallery is unavailable to a clinic. Citizens who never visit either reasonably ask why their taxes should support one rather than the other.*

*Even so, this trade-off is largely illusory at the scale involved. Cultural budgets form a very small fraction of national expenditure, whereas health and education absorb the overwhelming share of it. Redirecting the entire cultural budget would therefore extend a health service only briefly, while eliminating an entire sector permanently. Because the gain is marginal and the loss is total, the choice critics describe does not exist in the form they present it.*

*In conclusion, the benefits of public arts funding outweigh the opportunity cost it carries, because the amounts involved are too small to resolve the shortages they are blamed for. Ideally, governments should maintain cultural budgets while directing them towards work that would otherwise find no private support.*
